# AI Experiment Notes

## How I Used AI While Building This Project

This project was also an experiment in how AI can support learning and process design.

I did not use AI simply to generate a finished answer.

Instead, I used it as a thinking partner to explore ideas, challenge assumptions, structure information, and iterate on the workflow.

---

# Starting Point

My starting question was:

> How can someone transitioning from Educational Coordination into Customer Success build a practical understanding of customer onboarding?

Instead of only reading about Customer Success, I decided to build a hypothetical onboarding workflow.

---

# Experiment 1: Understanding the Problem

I started by breaking the onboarding process into questions:

- What happens after a customer purchases a product?
- What information should be transferred from Sales?
- How can a CSM understand customer goals?
- What does First Value mean?
- What signals might indicate risk?

AI helped me explore these concepts and organize them into a structured sequence.

However, I reviewed and challenged the suggestions rather than treating them as automatically correct.

---

# Experiment 2: Designing the Workflow

I experimented with different ways to structure the customer journey.

The workflow eventually developed into six stages:

1. Customer Handoff
2. Welcome and Discovery
3. Implementation
4. First Value
5. Adoption
6. Risk Detection

The goal was to create a simple framework that connected individual customer actions with broader Customer Success outcomes.

---

# Experiment 3: Applying the Workflow

I then created a hypothetical customer scenario.

This helped me test whether the workflow made practical sense.

Instead of asking:

> Does this process sound good?

I asked:

> What would a CSM actually need to do at each stage?

That led to more specific questions, possible customer risks, and potential actions.

---

# Experiment 4: Where AI Adds Value

While building the project, I explored several possible AI use cases.

For example:

## Before Meetings

AI could summarize customer context and previous conversations.

## After Meetings

AI could organize notes, action items, and follow-ups.

## Customer Education

AI could help create educational resources and support materials.

## Feedback Analysis

AI could help identify recurring patterns across customer feedback.

The important limitation is that AI output still requires context and human judgment.

---

# What I Learned

The most useful lesson from this experiment was that AI becomes more valuable when the question becomes more specific.

Generic prompts tend to produce generic answers.

The quality improves when you provide:

- Clear context
- A specific problem
- Constraints
- Desired outcomes

I also learned that building something is different from asking AI to explain something.

The process of creating this project forced me to make decisions, identify gaps in my knowledge, and think more critically about how Customer Success workflows operate.

---

# Next Experiment

My next step is to explore how this workflow could become more operational.

Some ideas include:

# Final Reflection

This project represents an early stage of my Customer Success learning journey.

I do not claim to have real-world Customer Success experience simply because I built this project.

Instead, I created it to learn by doing.

My goal is to continue combining my background in education, training, and people development with new knowledge in Customer Success, technology, and AI.
