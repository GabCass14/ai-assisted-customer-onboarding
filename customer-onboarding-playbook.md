# Customer Onboarding Playbook

## A Practical Customer Success Scenario

This playbook is an independent exercise designed to apply Customer Success concepts to a practical onboarding scenario.

The goal is to move beyond theory and think through the decisions a Customer Success Manager might make during a customer's first 30 days.

---

# The Scenario

## The Customer

Imagine a growing company that has recently purchased a workflow management platform.

The company currently manages several internal processes manually using:

- Spreadsheets
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Manual follow-ups

Their main goal is to create more structured and repeatable processes.

## The Challenge

The customer purchased the platform, but purchasing a product does not guarantee adoption.

The Customer Success challenge is to help the customer move from:

> "We bought the software."

to:

> "We are using the software to solve a real problem."

---

# Customer Profile

## Company

Growing service company with approximately 50 employees.

## Primary Contact

Operations Manager.

## Main Goal

Reduce manual work and create a more structured workflow for recurring processes.

## Potential Challenge

The team is used to existing manual processes and may resist changing their routines.

---

# The First 30 Days

## Day 0 — Sales to Customer Success Handoff

Before contacting the customer, the Customer Success Manager should understand:

- Why the customer purchased the product
- What problem they are trying to solve
- Who the key stakeholders are
- What expectations were created during the sales process
- What success could look like

### CSM Question

> What problem did the customer believe this product would solve?

---

# Day 1 — Welcome and Alignment

The first interaction should establish a clear path.

The CSM should:

- Welcome the customer
- Introduce the onboarding process
- Explain the next steps
- Confirm the customer's goals
- Identify important stakeholders

### Desired Outcome

The customer understands what will happen next.

---

# Days 1–7 — Discovery

The goal is to understand the customer's real workflow.

The CSM should ask questions such as:

- How does the current process work?
- Where does the team lose time?
- What information is frequently missed?
- Who is responsible for each step?
- What would improvement look like?

### Important Principle

Do not start with:

> "Here are all the features."

Start with:

> "What are you trying to achieve?"

---

# Days 7–14 — Implementation

The customer begins building their first workflow.

The CSM should help them focus on one meaningful use case instead of trying to implement everything at once.

Example:

## Before

A recurring process is managed through:

- A spreadsheet
- Multiple emails
- Manual reminders

## After

The process is structured inside the platform with:

- Clear steps
- Assigned responsibilities
- Due dates
- Automated reminders

---

# First Value

## Definition

For this customer, First Value happens when:

> The team successfully uses the platform to complete a recurring process that was previously managed manually.

This is more meaningful than:

- Logging in
- Watching a tutorial
- Creating an account

The customer experiences a practical improvement.

---

# Days 14–30 — Adoption

After First Value, the goal is to help the customer build consistent usage.

The CSM should monitor:

- Are users actively using the platform?
- Are workflows being completed?
- Has the primary contact engaged with the process?
- Are additional team members involved?

The objective is to move from:

> "We tried it."

to:

> "This is now part of how we work."

---

# Risk Signals

Potential risks might include:

## Risk 1 — No Activity

The customer has not completed important onboarding actions.

### Possible CSM Action

Reach out proactively to understand what is blocking progress.

---

## Risk 2 — Only One Active User

The primary contact is using the product, but the rest of the team is not.

### Possible CSM Action

Explore whether additional training or stakeholder involvement is necessary.

---

## Risk 3 — No First Value

The customer has completed setup but has not achieved a meaningful outcome.

### Possible CSM Action

Revisit the customer's original goal and simplify the next steps.

# How AI Could Support the CSM

## Before a Meeting

AI could help summarize:

- Previous conversations
- Customer goals
- Open questions
- Previous action items

---

## After a Meeting

AI could help structure:

- Meeting summaries
- Action items
- Responsibilities
- Follow-up communication

---

## Customer Education

AI could help create:

- Personalized learning materials
- FAQs
- Step-by-step guides
- Training resources

However, the CSM should review the output before sending it to the customer.

# My Key Takeaway

While building this scenario, I kept returning to one idea:

> Customer Success is not about making sure customers receive information. It is about helping them achieve outcomes.

This connects strongly with my background in education.

In education, giving someone information does not guarantee learning.

Similarly, in Customer Success, giving someone access to a product does not guarantee value.

In both cases, the challenge is helping people move from access to meaningful outcomes.
